Winding Switching for Flux Weakening of an Induction Motor

Summary

What

Simulation study of how switching the stator winding connection changes flux linkage and induced voltage of a three-phase induction motor at 1000 rpm.

Role

Modelled the lab induction motor in 2D in ANSYS Maxwell and compared base, differential and cumulative winding connections by flux linkage and induced voltage, in a team of 6.

Results

  1. Peak flux linkage of about 0.16 Wb (base), 0.04 Wb (differential) and 0.125 Wb (cumulative).
  2. Peak induced voltage of about 75 V, 35 V and 55 V in the same order.
  3. Speed was fixed at 1000 rpm in every case, so it is an input.

Problem

Flux weakening lets a motor run at higher speed without exceeding voltage limits. The task was to model the lab three-phase induction motor in 2D and apply winding switching to see how flux, voltage and torque change.

Method

  1. Built a 2D motor model from the given stator and rotor dimensions, with a Steel-1010 stator and an aluminum rotor, and ran it in ANSYS Maxwell at a reference speed of 1000 rpm.
  2. Simulated the base model first, then applied differential and cumulative winding switching and compared torque, flux linkage and induced voltage.
  3. Revised the design parameters from the originals after the first results did not match expected trends. The original graphs are kept in a closing section.

Equations

  1. EQ. 1Analytical model in the report, written for a compound DC motor: Phi = K_sh I_sh +/- K_se Ia; Te = Kt Phi Ia; omega = (V - Ia (Ra + Rse)) / (Ke Phi); Eb = Ke Phi omega

Results

The base model gives a flux linkage peak of about 0.16 Wb and an induced voltage peak of about 75 V. Differential switching gives a flux linkage peak just under 0.04 Wb and a voltage peak of about 35 V. Cumulative switching gives about 0.125 Wb and about 55 V. Torque oscillates between positive and negative values in all cases: about 2 Nm to below minus 10 Nm for the base model, about 1 Nm to minus 5 Nm for differential switching and about 10.15 Nm to minus 40 Nm for cumulative switching. The quoted torque ranges do not show a clear gain from switching, so none is claimed.

Limitations

  • Simulation only, in 2D, with no hardware test.
  • Design parameters were changed from the original brief after the first results looked unrealistic.
  • The report's equations describe a compound DC motor, and its literature review covers permanent magnet machines.

Tools

  • ANSYS Maxwell
