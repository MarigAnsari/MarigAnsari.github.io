Summary

What

Simulation study of how switching the stator winding connection changes flux linkage and induced voltage of a three‑phase induction motor at 1000 rpm.

Key finding

Differential winding switching cut peak flux linkage from about 0.16 to 0.04 Wb and induced voltage from 75 to 35 V, after a first unrealistic parameter set was revised.

Results

  1. Peak flux linkage of about 0.16 Wb (base), 0.04 Wb (differential) and 0.125 Wb (cumulative); peak induced voltage of about 75 V, 35 V and 55 V in the same order.
  2. The torque ranges do not show a consistent gain.

Problem

Flux weakening lets a motor run at higher speed without exceeding voltage limits. The task was to model the lab three‑phase induction motor in 2D and apply winding switching to see how flux, voltage and torque change.

Method

  1. Built a 2D motor model from the given stator and rotor dimensions, with a Steel‑1010 stator and an aluminum rotor, and ran it in ANSYS Maxwell. Speed was fixed at 1000 rpm in every case, so speed is an input.
  2. Simulated the base model first, then applied differential and cumulative winding switching and compared torque, flux linkage and induced voltage.
  3. Revised the design parameters from the originals after the first results did not match expected trends. The original graphs are kept in a closing section.

Equations

  1. EQ. 1

    Analytical model in the report, written for a compound DC motor:

    Φ=KshIsh±KseIa
    Te=KtΦIa
    ω=V−Ia(Ra+Rse)KeΦ
    Eb=KeΦω

Results

The base model gives a flux linkage peak of about 0.16 Wb and an induced voltage peak of about 75 V. Differential switching gives a flux linkage peak just under 0.04 Wb and a voltage peak of about 35 V. Cumulative switching gives about 0.125 Wb and about 55 V. Torque oscillates between positive and negative values in all cases: about 2 N·m to below −10 N·m for the base model, about 1 N·m to −5 N·m for differential switching and about 10 N·m to −40 N·m for cumulative switching. The torque ranges do not show a consistent gain.

Limitations

  • Simulation only, in 2D, with no hardware test.
  • Design parameters were changed from the original brief after the first results looked unrealistic.
  • The report's equations describe a compound DC motor, and its literature review covers permanent magnet machines.

Tools

  • ANSYS Maxwell
