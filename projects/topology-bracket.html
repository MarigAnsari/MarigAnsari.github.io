Summary

What

Sized the wall thickness of a Ti‑6Al‑4V cantilever bracket under combined loads, compared two side profiles, and ran FEA and a topology study.

Key challenge

Under 15 kN and 8 kN loads at a 600 MPa limit, the side profile decided the design: 14.5 mm instead of 18.5 mm.

Results

  1. Ti‑6Al‑4V bracket under 15 kN and 8 kN loads, with factor of safety 1.5 and a 600 MPa design stress limit.
  2. Topology study of a 150 × 100 × 50 mm block in SolidWorks.

Problem

A titanium cantilever bracket must carry a 15 kN axial load and an 8 kN transverse load without exceeding the von Mises limit and with deformation under 0.5 mm. The project sizes the thickness by hand, compares two side profiles, then runs FEA and a topology study on a solid block.

Method

  1. Material Ti‑6Al‑4V: Young's modulus 110 GPa, yield 900 MPa, UTS 950 MPa and density 4.43 g/cm³. A factor of safety of 1.5 gives a 600 MPa allowable stress.
  2. Part 1: the wall thickness of the hollow beam, found from the bending stress of each load, superposition, shear stress and von Mises stress, iterated in Excel until von Mises stress sat close to 600 MPa. Deformation and strength to weight ratio were checked.
  3. Part 2: derived von Mises stress as a function of thickness for two side profile variations, iterated the side profile thickness in Excel and selected the variation needing the lower thickness.
  4. Part 3: FEA of the selected variation, and a topology study of a solid 150 × 100 × 50 mm cantilever block in SolidWorks with the given loads, fixtures and mesh.

Limitations

  • Sizing rests on beam theory and Excel iteration.
  • No numeric FEA stress values and no mass saving figure are stated in the report text.
  • Single‑author coursework with no experiment.
