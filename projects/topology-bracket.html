Topology Optimisation of a Cantilever Bracket Structure

Domain
Structural design optimisation
Year
2024
Team
Individual project
Course
ME-314 Mechanics of Solids

Summary

What

Sized the wall thickness of a Ti-6Al-4V cantilever bracket under combined loads, compared two side profiles, and ran FEA and a topology study.

Role

Sized the wall thickness of a Ti-6Al-4V bracket in Excel, compared two side profiles, then ran FEA and a SolidWorks topology study, as sole author.

Results

  1. Ti-6Al-4V bracket under 15 kN and 8 kN loads, with factor of safety 1.5 and a 600 MPa design stress limit.
  2. Two side profiles needed 18.5 mm and 14.5 mm thickness, and the 14.5 mm variation was chosen.
  3. Topology study of a 150 x 100 x 50 mm block in SolidWorks.

Problem

A titanium cantilever bracket must carry a 15 kN axial load and an 8 kN transverse load without exceeding the von Mises limit and with deformation under 0.5 mm. The project sizes the thickness by hand, compares two side profiles, then runs FEA and a topology study on a solid block.

Method

  1. Material Ti-6Al-4V: Young's modulus 110 GPa, yield 900 MPa, UTS 950 MPa and density 4.43 g/cm3. A factor of safety of 1.5 gives a 600 MPa allowable stress.
  2. Part 1: bending stress from each load, superposition, shear stress and von Mises stress, with wall thickness iterated in Excel until von Mises stress sat close to 600 MPa. Deformation and strength to weight ratio were checked.
  3. Part 2: derived von Mises stress as a function of thickness for two side profile variations, iterated thickness in Excel and selected the variation needing the lower thickness.
  4. Part 3: FEA of the selected variation, and a topology study of a solid 150 x 100 x 50 mm cantilever block in SolidWorks with the given loads, fixtures and mesh.

Equations

  1. EQ. 1sigma = M c / I, with I = (1/12) b h^3 and M = F L
  2. EQ. 2tau = V Q / (I t)
  3. EQ. 3sigma_e = sqrt(0.5 [(sxx - syy)^2 + (syy - szz)^2 + (szz - sxx)^2] + 3 (txy^2 + tyz^2 + txz^2)), reducing to sqrt(szz^2 + 3 (tyz^2 + txz^2))
  4. EQ. 4Corrected yield strength = yield strength / factor of safety
  5. EQ. 5delta = F L / (A E)
  6. EQ. 6Strength to weight ratio = corrected yield strength / mass

Results

Part 1 selected a wall thickness of 0.525 mm, with von Mises stress close to the 600 MPa corrected yield strength and deformation far below 0.5 mm. In Part 2, variation 1 needs 18.5 mm and variation 2 needs 14.5 mm, so variation 2 was chosen. Part 3 gives a SolidWorks stress and displacement plot of the chosen variation and a topology study that marks material to keep and to remove, both shown as figures.

Validation

Part 3 runs FEA on the variation chosen in Part 2, with stress and displacement values shown in figures.

Limitations

  • Sizing rests on beam theory and Excel iteration.
  • No numeric FEA stress values and no mass saving figure are stated in the report text.
  • Single-author coursework with no experiment.

Tools

  • ANSYS
  • SolidWorks Simulation
  • SolidWorks
  • Excel
